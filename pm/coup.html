01The business

A service people loved, on an app that let them down.

Coup ran electric, free-floating mopeds in three cities, backed by Bosch and growing quickly. The app had been built to get the fleet on the street fast, and it showed: riders were missing information at the moments they needed it.

Behind it, field operations ran on WhatsApp and spreadsheets, and every moped stuck off the road was lost revenue.

Project outline

The ride first. Then the operation behind it.

The first job was the rider app. The second was the part riders never saw: the dispatchers and field workers keeping the fleet running.

02The ride

Interviews in three cities kept returning one word.

The word was confidence. Riders wanted to know they’d picked the right moped, what the app was doing, and what to do when something went wrong. So we rebuilt every step of the ride around one question: what now?

Bluetooth dropped and unlocks failed, and the old app left riders guessing. We found each of those moments and rebuilt it around clear guidance and honest error messages.

The rebuilt ride: route, reservation, unlock and cancel.

03Cancellations

One in eight rides was cancelled. Nobody knew why.

Damaged moped, flat battery, missing helmet? Without a reason, every fix was a guess. So we added one step to cancelling: a short list of reasons, one tap, and you’re done. For the first time, operations had a timestamped record of why rides failed.

A separate reporting feature would have asked riders for extra effort. A step inside a flow they were already in cost them one tap.

A structured reason, asked at a moment riders were already in.

04The pilot

Test the expensive idea before building it.

Could riders swap flat batteries themselves for ride credit, and cut operating costs? Proving it properly needed charging infrastructure that didn’t exist yet, so we ran a small pilot inside the app and watched what riders did. That gave the business enough to decide without building anything.

The battery swap pilot: testing intent before infrastructure.

The pilot’s job was to answer a question, not to ship a feature.

05Ops tools

No brief, so we started with the work itself.

Jobs arrived through group chats, repairs were logged if at all, and dispatchers had no live view of the fleet. So we rode along with field workers and sat with dispatchers. Dispatchers needed to see and steer the fleet; field workers needed clear jobs and a quick way to close them.

That became two connected products: a web dashboard for dispatch, and a mobile app for the field built for rain, winter and gloved hands.
