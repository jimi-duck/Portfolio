Digital health · Founding team · 3 min read

Vivy

A health record patients carry on their phone, with doctors and an insurer on the other side. As one of the first designers, I worked on all three.

Product Designer, founding team

2017–2019

Allianz · Engineering

Patient app · Practitioner portal · Invoice claims

The problem
In Germany, medical records are scattered across practices, paper files and fax machines. A digital record only works if patients, doctors and insurers all take part.
What I did
Designed the core flow for requesting and sharing records and the portal doctors answered from, and ran a four-day sprint with Allianz to define invoice claims.
The result
A request flow that stayed honest when doctors were slow to reply, and an invoice feature with requirements design, engineering and Allianz agreed on.

01The business

Your records exist. Just not anywhere useful.

Lab results sit with one provider, X-rays with another, discharge letters in a folder at home. Vivy, backed by Allianz, set out to give patients one encrypted place to keep them, request new ones and share them with any doctor they chose.

Joining as one of the first designers meant more than screens: setting up research, agreeing principles, and building a shared way of making decisions in a heavily regulated industry.

Project outline

One record, three sides. All of them had to take part.

The record only works if doctors answer and the insurer joins in. The chapters follow those sides, and a launch where one of them wasn’t ready.

02The patient

Request it, receive it, share it. Encrypted throughout.

A patient asks their GP for last month’s blood results and gets them on their phone, with no calls, no waiting room and no paper. They can share the file with any practitioner in seconds, and they alone decide who sees it.

An early flow map: how a request reaches a doctor, and where it could quietly stall. That risk turned out to be the real story.

03Launch

The technology worked. The ecosystem wasn’t ready.

At launch, many requests went unanswered because most doctors had never heard of Vivy, and patients took that to mean the app was broken. So instead of a spinner, the status screen said what was pending, why it might be late, and what the patient could do in the meantime.

Patients coped far better with honest information than with false confidence.

A specific status, a reason and a next step, where a spinner used to be.

04The insurer

Four days with Allianz. One feature, defined properly.

Private patients in Germany pay the bill first and claim it back later, which is slow, paper-heavy and stressful. I ran a four-day sprint in Munich with Allianz to understand both sides before anyone designed a screen, and it ended with requirements design, engineering and the insurer all agreed on.

05The practitioner

It only works if doctors trust it too.

I designed the practitioner side as well: a secure, direct connection that felt professional enough for a doctor to build into their working day.

The patient’s phone and the practitioner’s portal: two contexts, one trust model.
