Coup
Mobility

I helped Bosch’s e-moped service find out why rides were failing, test an expensive idea before paying for it, and give the field team tools that kept 5,000 mopeds on the road.

Senior Product Designer

2019–2020

Rider app · Dispatcher dashboard · Field worker app

The problem
The rider app left people stuck when a ride went wrong. Field operations ran on WhatsApp and spreadsheets.
What I did
Mapped where rides failed and rebuilt the app around it. Put answers where riders got stuck, and designed field tools from ride-alongs.
The result
A rebuilt app in three cities, a record of why rides were cancelled, and field work out of group chats.

01The rider app

Rebuilt around the ride, not reskinned.

Interviews in all three cities kept returning one word: confidence. Riders wanted to know they’d picked the right moped, what the app was doing, and what to do when something failed. So we rebuilt every step around one question: what now?

02Mapping the ride

Every step mapped, including the ones that fail.

I flowcharted the whole rider app, errors included. Ending a ride ran through seven systems, from the app to the scooter. Mapping every call between them showed which failures had no error message at all.

Booking a ride, with every error in orange.

Ending a ride. Existing errors on the left, missing ones on the right.

03Support

Every call cost €4.72. So help arrives before the call.

I asked for the raw call log: 205 calls a day, about €350k a year. Testing showed riders weren’t failing to find help. They weren’t looking. So the help had to come to them, at the step where each question comes up.

For the support team, it was their own log turned into answers, and any new failure had an obvious place to go.

05Ready? Then fold in the kickstand, squeeze one of the brakes and push the “go” button at the same time. Now slowly accelerate.

Shown at the start-riding step. Signed off by the hardware team.

Every answer is in the rider’s words, not the log’s, and in the order the hands do it: kickstand, brake, button.

Riders made 12 calls a day asking how to start, because nobody had written this down where they’d see it.

04Cancellations

One in eight rides was cancelled. Nobody knew why.

Damaged moped, flat battery, missing helmet? Without a reason, every fix was a guess. We added one step to cancelling: a short list of reasons, one tap, done. For the first time, ops had a timestamped record of why rides failed.

A structured reason, asked at a moment riders were already in.

05Testing an idea

Test the expensive idea before building it.

Could riders swap flat batteries themselves, for ride credit? Proving it properly meant charging infrastructure that didn’t exist. So we ran a small in-app pilot and watched what riders actually did: enough for the business to decide without building anything.

Community battery swap pilot: testing intent before infrastructure.

06Field operations

No brief. So we started where the work happened.

Jobs arrived through group chats, repairs were logged if at all, and dispatchers had no live view of the fleet. There was no brief, so we started by riding along with field workers and sitting with dispatchers. Two needs came out of it, and two products.

The field app had to work in rain, traffic and winter, often one-handed and in gloves. Every control is large, obvious and hard to mistake.
