01Roam

Discover a city, one run at a time.

Choose how far you want to run and what you’d like to see. Roam builds a runnable loop past the sights.

No account. No GPS tracking. No social feed. Just a different way to explore.

Open Roam ↗︎
The brief
When I’m somewhere new, often I want to head out for a run and explore the local area, not spend twenty minutes planning a route.
The challenge
Route generation without the bloat. Major running apps are cluttered with GPS telemetry, logins, and social feeds; the goal was delivering instant, runnable routes with zero tracking and lightweight SVG rendering that opens in seconds.
Built with
Expo Web · TypeScript · SVG map rendering

Three questions, then a run.

Roam opens on a map of Berlin, with no account and no location prompt. It asks where you start, how far you want to go, and what would make the run great.

Three loops, planned on the phone.

Roam plans routes on the device, from OpenStreetMap and Wikidata for Berlin. For this run it offered three loops of 7.7 to 8.1 km. The best match passes 10 sights, from the Fernsehturm to the New Synagogue.

Stop by stop, before you go.

“Explore the run” turns the route into its 10 stops. Each one says which side it’s on, how far off the path, how many minutes in, and what’s underfoot.

One run in Roam: 8 km from Rosenthaler Platz, sights first. Real screens; sight photos show the app’s map placeholder

02Wayward

An ore hauler, a buried secret, a long way to go.

A 3D space-survival space opera built for mobile and desktop browsers. You pilot an aging ore hauler on the run from the authorities: mine asteroids for fuel, refit your ship, and try to stay one jump ahead.

Play Wayward ↗︎
The brief
A 3D space game with a story behind it, that runs in a phone browser as well as on a desktop.
The hard part
Getting the story right. Testing each draft by playing it, and pushing the AI until the plot and the game work.
Built with
TypeScript and Three.js. Its own repo, deployed on its own.

03Erntereif

A seasonal calendar for what’s growing near you.

Pick a month and it shows what’s being harvested, what’s out of cold store, and what the imported version costs in carbon. 87 crops, five countries, German interface.

Open Erntereif ↗︎
The brief
When I’m food shopping, I want to know what’s in season here right now, so I can buy that instead of something flown in.
The hard part
Data integrity and QA. Generative tools draft data in seconds, but reviewing 87 crops meant wrestling with the real question: fact-checking every item by hand versus learning where to trust AI-generated content.
Built with
React and TypeScript.

04Tanken

Fuel prices, and whether to fill up now or wait.

German stations have had to report their prices publicly since 2013. Tanken reads that feed and shows today’s average, how it got there, the five biggest chains ranked cheapest first, and a guarded outlook for tomorrow.

The brief
When I drive past a petrol station, I want to know whether today’s price is a good one, so I don’t fill up the day before it drops.
The hard part
The API hunt. Finding, connecting, and wrangling Germany’s public fuel feeds to pull the raw numbers needed for clean historical trends and a reliable forecast.
Built with
SwiftUI. One screen, no tab bar.
