01Wayward

An ore hauler, a buried secret, a long way to go.

You’ve stolen the Wayward, an old government ore hauler with an archive aboard that proves the jump-lane blackout seventy years ago was a purge. Mine Cyrite, refine it into jump fluid, refit the ship, and stay ahead of the Offices of Peace across eight regions.

Play Wayward ↗︎
The brief
A 3D roguelite with a story behind it, that runs in a phone browser as well as on a desktop.
The hard part
Balance. A scripted pilot plays whole journeys and writes a report, so a change shows up in the numbers before I play it.
Built with
TypeScript and Three.js. Its own repo, deployed on its own.

02Erntereif

A seasonal calendar for what’s growing near you.

Pick a month and it shows what’s being harvested, what’s out of cold store, and what the imported version costs in carbon. 87 crops, five countries, German interface.

Open Erntereif ↗︎
The brief
When I’m food shopping, I want to know what’s in season here right now, so I can buy that instead of something flown in.
The hard part
Making 87 images look like one set. All of them are cut from one generated contact sheet, so they share a light and an angle.
Built with
React and TypeScript.

September in Germany. 65 varieties harvested, 66 available once cold store counts

March, the same page. Nine harvested against September’s 65

Every crop carries a cooking note

03Tanken

Fuel prices, and whether to fill up now or wait.

German stations have had to report their prices publicly since 2013. Tanken reads that feed and shows today’s average, how it got there, the five biggest chains ranked cheapest first, and a guarded outlook for tomorrow.

The brief
When I drive past a petrol station, I want to know whether today’s price is a good one, so I don’t fill up the day before it drops.
The hard part
Being straight about the forecast. A feed it can’t reach says so, and lowers the reliability instead of being guessed at.
Built with
SwiftUI. One screen, no tab bar.
